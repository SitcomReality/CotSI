**Lean into a “remnant cosmos” language: faceted, faintly prismatic void-relic chrome that lets the low-poly world stay the star while the UI feels like leftover divine geometry.**

The current UI is a competent dark-strategy overlay (near-black rectangles, thin gold, functional sans, standard pills and cards). It reads as a generic modern HUD dropped on top of a colorful, cartoony, low-poly world. The mismatch is the darkness + flatness + rectangularity versus the world’s saturation, chunky lighting, and hexagonal nature. The lore (dead/dying gods, Interregnum, relics, Rainbow Aftermath, 7-fold Paley structure) gives you a perfect excuse to make the chrome itself feel like salvaged god-stuff rather than an app skin.

### Unifying idea
Treat every panel, bar, and modal as a **shard, scrying tablet, or heptagonal reliquary** floating in the afterglow of vanished gods. Deep, saturated cosmic dusk (not near-black) with iridescent/rainbow-leak accents, faceted or heptagonal framing, and the seven faction colors used as living materials instead of just text tints. The map stays vibrant and readable; the UI recedes into colored dusk and only flares when it needs attention. This keeps the cartoon energy (bold, simple shapes, cheerful saturation) while giving the “high-fantasy dead gods” melancholy without going grimdark or muddy.

### Color palette
- **Base / dusk**: Rich, saturated darks — deep indigo-violet, twilight plum, or inked teal-black — instead of flat navy/black. Subtle nebula or faint hex/star noise so panels feel like atmosphere, not a solid fill. Avoid pure #000 or desaturated charcoal.
- **Divine metal**: Keep and warm up the gold/amber you already use for primary actions (End Turn, Inscribe, artifact cards). Make it feel like tarnished orichalcum or captured sunlight, with a slight inner glow.
- **Faction chroma as material**: The seven colors (Crucible orange-red, Verdant green, etc.) should appear as glows, borders, ink, or thin prismatic edges, not only as name colors. A selected champion or active event can tint its local chrome. Let faint spectral leaks or iridescent edges appear on important frames and the heptagram.
- **Text and contrast**: Warm off-white or cream for body, not stark white. Faction names and key numbers stay in their chroma. High enough contrast for strategy readability, but never sterile.
- **World bleed**: Semi-transparent panels (or very dark ones with colored inner glows) so the map’s lighting and palette remain visible. The UI should feel like it’s sitting in the same air as the hexes.

Result: the screen stays colorful even when UI is up, and the “dead gods” theme becomes leftover light rather than generic darkness.

### Layout philosophy
Keep the information architecture you already have (persistent heptagram + log on the right, champion sheet bottom-left, top status, minimap) but stop boxing everything into axis-aligned dark rectangles.

- **Hex / heptagon grammar**: Use 30/60° cuts, chamfered or fully hexagonal/heptagonal frames, and alignment that echoes the map and the Paley diagram. Buttons and cards can be rounded hexes or have faceted corners. The heptagram itself can feel like the visual keystone — larger, more luminous, or treated as a physical object (a hanging star-map or cracked tablet) rather than a small diagram in a box.
- **Less chrome, more map**: Give the world more breathing room. Side panels can be narrower, more translucent, or able to recede. Contextual info (selected hex, combat preview) can appear as a small floating shard near the action instead of always occupying a full column.
- **Asymmetry and relic placement**: The champion panel can feel like a personal altar or portable reliquary (HP as a glowing well, relics as inset objects). The top bar can be a thin constellation or hanging fragment rather than a flat strip of pills. The minimap can be a scrying shard or a cracked mosaic.
- **Modals as artifacts**: The “First Illumination” card-choice overlay is currently a standard dark dialog. Make choice moments feel like examining glowing relics on a stone or in a beam of leftover light — still clear and readable, just not a floating Windows card.

The 7-fold / Paley structure is a gift; repeat heptagonal rhythm and “each beats three” visual language in frames and even in how you group information so the UI itself teaches the combat system.

### General style / visual language
- **Faceted + luminous, not ornate-for-its-own-sake**: Match the low-poly world with angular, chunky frames and simple geometric decoration (runes that look carved or inlaid, not filigree). Add soft inner glows, thin iridescent edges, and very restrained particle dust or fading-god motes. It should feel handmade and a bit toy-like, not AAA grim or corporate-flat.
- **Cartoon readability**: Bold silhouettes, limited decoration, strong color blocking. Icons should look like they belong next to the 3D characters (simple, slightly chunky, maybe a hint of the same lighting). Avoid both tiny filigree and sterile Material Design.
- **Typography**: A distinctive but highly readable display face for titles and the heptagram labels (geometric or lightly celestial, not blackletter). Clean, slightly rounded or humanist sans for body and numbers so it stays friendly and cartoony. Hierarchy through size, color, and glow rather than extra boxes.
- **Materials and motion**: Panels can have a faint “tablet / stained-glass / captured-light” quality. Hover and state changes use color shifts and soft pulses rather than just brightness. The heptagram and relic choices are natural places for satisfying, readable animation.
- **Tone balance**: Cosmic and a little mournful in the dusk and gold, playful in the saturation and chunky shapes. It should feel like the gods died and left a beautiful, slightly broken toy box, not a spreadsheet or a crypt.

This direction keeps your existing information density and the strengths of the screenshots (clear faction colors, the heptagram as a unique centerpiece, gold CTAs) while making the chrome feel like it was designed for *this* world instead of dropped on top of it. The map remains the vibrant, cartoony heart; the UI becomes the colored dusk and salvaged geometry around it.
