**A “prismatic remnant / dying-god geometry” overlay: dark cosmic void as the canvas, but every frame, accent, and interactive element is faceted, saturated, and hexagonal/heptagonal so the UI feels like leftover divine machinery rather than a generic dark-mode HUD.**

The current mismatch between the visuals of the game proper and the UI is clear. The world is already doing the hard work—warm low-poly lighting, long playful shadows, chunky trees/rocks, saturated faction colors (that orange mascot, the purple dunes, the green cacti). The UI is a standard navy-box strategy overlay (top ticker, floating stat card, event log, gold CTA). It reads as “functional Steam indie” instead of “the last flickering interface of dead gods after the cataclysmic divine war.”

### Core direction
Treat the HUD as **scrying shards and heptagram relics** floating in the void between gods. Not grimdark stone tablets, not clean sci-fi holograms—cartoony low-poly artifacts that still glow with leftover supernal light. The 7-node Paley tournament and the hex map become the visual language of the entire chrome.

- **Color palette**: Keep a deep, rich void (indigo-to-near-black with a hint of nebula purple, not flat #111) so the 3D map stays the hero. Then explode saturation on everything interactive. Pull the exact 7 faction hues from the heptagram (the orange CRU, purple REV, green VER, cyan ARC, gold HRT, magenta MSK, pale HOL) and use them as living accents—borders, glows, potency dots, log icons. Gold/amber stays the “divine remnant” color for primary actions (End Turn, Inscribe, important numbers) but make it warmer and more metallic, with a faint rainbow sheen at the edges. Text is cream/off-white with a very soft bloom, never pure sterile white. Subtle iridescence (thin oil-slick or stained-glass rims) on panels and buttons could convey the “Rainbow Aftermath” weather in the chrome without turning everything into a rave.
- **Layout philosophy**: Hex and heptagon everything that can take it. Player card, stat blocks, and the artifact modal become beveled low-poly crystals or floating hex tablets rather than rounded rectangles. The Paley heptagram should feel like the sacred centerpiece it is—larger, more luminous, perhaps with faint constellation lines or a slow pulse. Group information into “relic clusters” instead of stacked lists: the left champion panel as your personal altar/shrine, the right side as the cosmic ledger (heptagram + chronicle). Give the minimap a crystalline or scrying-pool treatment so it doesn’t look like a leftover debug widget. Keep the functional bones (top ticker, bottom-left actions) but let negative space and geometric framing breathe; the current panels feel a bit bolted-on.
- **General style**: Faceted, chunky, slightly toy-like to match the world’s low-poly charm. Frames have visible bevels and catch the same warm light as the terrain. Icons are simple geometric or tiny low-poly illustrations (not generic line icons). Buttons and cards have a gentle “carved crystal” or “stained-glass shard” quality—playful rather than solemn. The event log becomes a living chronicle: short illustrated entries or colored runes instead of plain text. Modals (the First Illumination choice is a good start) should feel ceremonial—two glowing relics presented on a heptagonal dais, not two stacked cards in a dark box. Micro-motion is important: soft prismatic glints, particles like fading stardust or crumbling god-dust, a little squash-and-stretch on hovers so it stays cartoony.

This keeps the UI readable and strategy-friendly while making it feel like it belongs to the same universe: the colorful, slightly melancholy playground left after the gods died. The world stays vibrant and inviting; the chrome becomes the mysterious, geometric, faintly decaying frame around it.

The remnant-geometry approach is the cleanest marriage of the two aesthetics you already have. The existing gold End Turn button and the heptagram diagram are already pointing in the right direction—just let the rest of the interface catch up to the map’s personality.
